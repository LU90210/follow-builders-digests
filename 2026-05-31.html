Salesforce's Agentic Transformation with Claude Code

Salesforce published a detailed case study on going agentic with Claude Code, and the results are striking: a 231-day migration shipped in 13 days, one PR delivered 21 endpoints at 100% test coverage, and total incidents dropped 5% despite higher output. The cluster of builders around this story is less about celebrating the numbers and more about what they signal — that the teams seeing the biggest wins are completely changing how they work, not just speeding up what they already do.

The Rise of AI Agents and the Need for Guardians

As autonomous AI agents proliferate in enterprises — from coding assistants like Claude Code and Codex to low-code platforms — a new security paradigm is emerging. Onyx Security CEO Maxim Bar Kogan argues that traditional security tools (identity, endpoint, API security) are ill-equipped for agents that need broad permissions to be useful, and that enterprises now need specialized 'guardian' agents trained to oversee other agents. The consensus among builders is that agentic adoption is outpacing security controls, creating both risk and opportunity.

Maxim Bar Kogan

Co-founder & CEO, Onyx Security

The pivotal insight driving Onyx Security is that AutoGPT gave everyone a glimpse of a future where models decide what to do and then execute it in a loop — and that future is now arriving with Claude Code, Cowork, and OpenCloud. Bar Kogan's team trains small, specialized models that act as a 'chess intuition' layer: they rapidly flag high-risk agent actions so that smarter oversight agents only need to intervene at critical moments. This keeps costs and latency low while catching dangerous behavior. The chess analogy is apt — grandmasters don't calculate every move, they intuit when to stop and think. Onyx's approach is similarly about efficient computation: spend intelligence where risk is high, not everywhere. The company also reveals that over 50% of enterprise AI usage today is autonomous coding agents, with low-code automations at 45% and first-party custom agents at just 2% — a distribution that suggests most enterprises are adopting off-the-shelf agentic tools faster than they can build their own.

As you're exponentially doing more things with the AIs, you're going to start having really bad actions happen. And we've seen some of that happen lately with agents accidentally publishing code and tokens that they weren't supposed to.
