Jan Dubois

Co-leads Post-Training Frontiers team @OpenAI; previously Stanford Alpaca

OpenAI's Jan Dubois, who co-leads the Post-Training Frontiers team, explains why AI progress suddenly feels like a step function even though capability gains are continuous. The key threshold was reliability: around December 2024, OpenAI crossed the point where models could be trusted for sustained real-world work. Dubois breaks down three forces behind the acceleration: (1) reliability making tools actually usable, (2) AI-assisted coding speeding up internal R&D loops, and (3) reinforcement learning expanding from verifiable domains (math, coding competitions) into messy real-world tasks. On GPT 5.5, he's most proud of efficiency—most tasks now run ~2x faster—and the company-wide alignment required to ship it.

You need to reach this level of reliability to really make any of these AI tools very useful, and I think we just crossed that probably December last year, at least at OpenAI. Now we can trust these models to do a lot of the work that we are doing.
