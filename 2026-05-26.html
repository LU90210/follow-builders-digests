Ivan Burazin

CEO of Daytona, agent infrastructure startup

Ivan Burazin, CEO of Daytona, makes a first-principles case for why every AI agent needs its own sandbox—a dedicated, isolated computer environment. He frames agents as 'digital knowledge workers' that need real machines to install tools, execute code, browse the web, and operate securely without sharing credentials with their human operators. Daytona spins up sandboxes in 60 milliseconds and can launch 50,000 in under 90 seconds, with customers requesting up to 5 million concurrent instances. Burazin argues that current models don't actually learn on the job—they only retain context through memory—so the infrastructure layer (sandbox + tools + memory + orchestration) is where the real moat lies. He also warns that a global CPU shortage may arrive faster than people think, and that the current transformer/GPU paradigm may not be the end state of AI.

My argument is that every agent will need at least one sandbox, sometimes more.
