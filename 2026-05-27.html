Ivan Burazin

CEO of Daytona

The Takeaway: Agents are not just chatbots — they are digital knowledge workers that need their own computers (sandboxes) to do real work.

Ivan Burazin, CEO of Daytona, makes the case that the agent stack is still being assembled and sandboxes are its most underappreciated layer. He draws a sharp distinction between stateless cloud infrastructure (AWS, Azure) and the stateful, long-running environments agents need. The analogy is a truck versus a sports car: both have wheels and engines, but they are built for completely different jobs. Hyperscalers optimized for immutable app deployments cannot simply pivot to agent sandboxes without fundamental architectural changes.

Burazin shares a personal story that broke his trust in local-agent setups: he asked Claude to fetch bank data for a board presentation, and the agent replied, "just log in and give me access." That moment convinced him agents need isolated machines with their own accounts, phone numbers for 2FA, and spending limits — essentially treating them as digital employees.

On the technical side, Daytona threw out Kubernetes and built its own scheduler because existing orchestrators were designed for ephemeral workloads, not long-running agents that may need to persist for days. The company now supports multiple isolation primitives — Firecracker microVMs, QEMU, hardened containers — chosen automatically based on the agent's needs. Burazin also warns that a CPU shortage may follow the GPU crunch, because RL training and agent inference both consume enormous CPU capacity.

For go-to-market, he argues that technical founders often neglect distribution. His playbook is awareness + preference + deterministic differentiation. Even if a competitor matches your product, brand, community, and customer experience can still win. Daytona has zero salespeople and relies on PLG, fast support response times, and IRL events — culminating in a 4,000-person conference at Chase Center.

My argument is that every agent will need at least one sandbox, sometimes more.

Ivan Burazin

CEO of Daytona

核心观点：Agent 不只是聊天机器人，而是需要独立计算机（sandbox）来完成实际工作的数字知识工作者。

Daytona CEO Ivan Burazin 认为，agent 技术栈仍在组装中，而 sandbox 是最被低估的一层。他清晰区分了 Stateless 的云基础设施（AWS、Azure）与 agent 所需的 Stateful、长时间运行环境。他用卡车与跑车作类比：两者都有轮子和引擎，但为完全不同的任务而设计。为不可变应用部署优化的超大规模云厂商，无法在不改变基础架构的情况下直接转向 agent sandbox。

Burazin 分享了一个让他彻底放弃本地 agent 方案的故事：他曾让 Claude 去取银行数据用于董事会报告，agent 的回答是"只要登录并给我权限"。那一刻让他确信，agent 需要隔离的机器、自己的账户、用于双因素认证的手机号，以及消费限额——本质上要把它们当作数字员工来管理。

技术层面，Daytona 抛弃了 Kubernetes，自建调度器，因为现有编排工具为短暂负载设计，不适合需要持续运行数天的 agent。公司现在支持多种隔离原语——Firecracker microVM、QEMU、加固容器——根据 agent 需求自动选择。Burazin 还警告，CPU 短缺可能紧随 GPU 危机而来，因为 RL 训练和 agent 推理都会消耗巨量 CPU。

在市场推广方面，他认为技术型创始人往往忽视分发。他的 playbook 是：认知度 + 偏好 + 确定性差异化。即使对手产品与你相当，品牌、社区和客户体验仍能取胜。Daytona 零销售人员，靠 PLG、快速支持响应和线下活动获客—— culminated in a 4,000-person conference at Chase Center。

My argument is that every agent will need at least one sandbox, sometimes more.
