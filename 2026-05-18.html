Anthropic memory feature reactions

Anthropic shipped persistent memory yesterday. Three builders converge on the same critique: enterprise customers will struggle with the per-org isolation model, not the feature itself.

Aaron Levie

Box CEO

Box CEO Aaron Levie argues Anthropic's new memory feature solves a real problem at the technical level but creates a deployment problem at the enterprise level.

The per-org isolation model assumes each company is one tenant, but most Box customers have shared workspaces spanning multiple legal entities. He predicts enterprises will either build their own memory layer on top or wait for Anthropic to ship multi-tenant scoping.

Anthropic's memory is impressive but the per-org isolation will be a nightmare for shared workspaces.

On Deck Podcast

Episode: How Anthropic Scaled Engineering Org From 50 to 500

The Takeaway: Scaling an AI engineering org is a hiring-philosophy problem, not a recruiting-throughput problem.

Anthropic's head of engineering describes how the org grew 10x in 18 months without the usual quality dilution by anchoring on a 'taste' bar — candidates who could articulate why a feature shouldn't ship, not just how to ship it. The result: fewer technically-strong-but-direction-confused PRs, lower coordination cost.

Most memorable: the explicit rejection of the meta-FAANG playbook. 'We hired for taste, not just skill, because at 500 engineers your bottleneck becomes is this the right thing to build not can we build it.'
